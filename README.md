### 💡 Tips for Writing Tools
- **Be specific with your prompt** — instead of "write a blog post", say "write a 500-word blog post for a beginner audience about X"
- **Iterate** — ask the AI to rewrite in a different tone or style
- **Use Claude for long documents** — it handles large context better than most
- **Ask for outlines first** before generating full content

| **Flux** | Open-weight image model with great realism | Realistic images, open-source use | [flux.ai](https://blackforestlabs.ai) |

### 💡 Tips for Image Generation
- **Be descriptive** — include style, lighting, camera angle, mood, and medium (e.g. "oil painting", "photorealistic")
- **Use negative prompts** — tell the model what you *don't* want
- **Aspect ratio matters** — specify portrait, landscape, or square depending on use case
- **Iterate quickly** — generate multiple variations and refine the best one

## 🧠 Prompting Tips & Best Practices

Getting the most out of any AI tool comes down to how you prompt it. Here are universal tips:

### The Basics
- **Be specific** — vague prompts get vague answers
- **Provide context** — who you are, what the output is for, and who the audience is
- **Set the format** — ask for bullet points, tables, a numbered list, or plain prose
- **Specify length** — "in 3 sentences" or "in under 200 words" keeps outputs concise

### Advanced Techniques
- **Role prompting** — "Act as an expert financial analyst and..."
- **Chain of thought** — "Think step by step before giving your answer"
- **Few-shot prompting** — provide 1-2 examples of the output you want before asking
- **Iterative refinement** — treat it like a conversation; ask for changes on the output

### Example Prompt Template
```
Context: [Who you are / what the project is]
Task: [What you want the AI to do]
Format: [How you want the output structured]
Tone: [Formal / casual / technical / friendly]
Length: [Word count or rough size]
```
